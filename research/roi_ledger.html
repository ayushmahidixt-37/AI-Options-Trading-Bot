Profit % ≠ win %. Win rate is the share of trades that closed positive. Profit % (ROI) is net profit or loss divided by the total rupees actually turned over on entries that period. A strategy can have a low win rate and still be strongly profitable (small frequent losses, occasional large wins), or a high win rate and a mediocre return (many small wins, one large loss) -- the two numbers answer different questions, so this table always shows both, never one standing in for the other.
Daily Profit % is a simple average, not a compounding rate. It is Profit % (ROI) divided by the number of actual NSE trading days the run's date range spans (not calendar days, and not just days a trade happened to fire) -- e.g. a run showing 4.55% ROI over 39 trading days shows roughly 0.117% per day. This is not the same as saying "and it compounds daily" (like a CAGR figure would imply) -- these backtests use a fixed lot size per trade, not full reinvestment of prior profits into larger positions, so a flat average is the honest way to read "roughly how much per trading day," while a compounded figure would overstate what actually happens. Use it to compare pace across runs of very different lengths (a 3-month sweep vs. a 19-month full-history run), not as a growth-rate prediction.

Notes on this table

  • "Period" is the run's own backtest date range (its Development window, Validation window, a single quarter, or a full continuous range) -- not how long any individual trade was held. Individual trades close same-day or roll to force-exit; "Days" here is the calendar span of the backtest itself. "Daily Profit %" instead divides by actual NSE trading days in that range (weekends/holidays excluded, from the underlying's own candle calendar) -- see the callout above for why it's a simple average, not a compounding rate.
  • Chunked vs. continuous ranges: some groups run one strategy's fixed parameters across several separate quarter-long calls; the "full-range continuous runs" group runs the same final configs as one continuous pass instead. The two don't sum to identical totals -- a fresh quarter-chunked call restarts each indicator's warm-up window at the quarter boundary, while a continuous run carries that warm-up through. The continuous version is the more realistic of the two.
  • "Invested" is capital turned over, not capital at risk simultaneously: each strategy holds one position at a time, so this is the sum of entry premium × lot size across all trades in that run -- total rupees put to work over the period, not peak simultaneous exposure.
  • Rows tagged rejected are strategies this project's own research explicitly rejected -- shown here for completeness, not as a recommendation.
  • Keeping this table current: see research/roi_ledger.py -- call record_run(group, label, start, end, result) right after any backtest worth keeping, then re-run this script. New group names not yet in this script's curated list still render correctly with a generic label.
